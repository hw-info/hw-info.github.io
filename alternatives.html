MSI App Player alternatives: other ways to run Android games on a PC

The main MSI App Player alternatives are BlueStacks itself, Google Play Games for PC, independent emulators such as LDPlayer, MEmu and NoxPlayer, and developer tools like the Android Studio Emulator. Here is what each one is for.

Why look for MSI App Player alternatives

People look for MSI App Player alternatives for a few clear reasons. MSI lists no Mac version. MSI publishes no requirements or release notes. The emulator can clash with Hyper-V and other virtual machine tools. Some users simply want a game catalogue run by Google. Before switching, check whether your problem is the virtualization conflict. If it is, the Cannot start MSI App Player fix may solve it without changing emulators.

Every option on this page except cloud play also relies on hardware virtualization, so switching emulators will not get around a PC that has it locked off.

BlueStacks 5: the closest match

Because MSI built App Player with BlueStacks, BlueStacks 5 is the nearest substitute. You get the same engine and a Multi-Instance Manager, plus published minimums (4 GB RAM, 5 GB disk), release notes and a macOS version. You lose MSI's Console Mode branding and the MSI download page. MSI App Player vs BlueStacks 5 compares them in detail.

Google Play Games for PC

Google's own program runs a selection of Android games on Windows, with sign in through your Google account. It is not a full Android environment. You get the games Google has approved for PC, not every app in the Play Store. Choose it if your games are in its catalogue and you prefer software from Google. It also needs virtualization enabled.

Independent emulators and developer tools

LDPlayer, MEmu Play and NoxPlayer are free Android emulators from independent vendors, aimed at mobile gamers on Windows. They compete with BlueStacks rather than build on it, so settings and features differ. Read each vendor's own pages for what is free and what is optional before you install.

The Android Studio Emulator is Google's tool for app developers. It is accurate and up to date with Android releases, but it is built for testing apps, not for playing games with a controller. Waydroid runs Android in a container on Linux, which neither MSI nor BlueStacks supports.

Which to pick

  • You own MSI hardware or a Claw and want controller play: stay with MSI App Player, covered in the MSI App Player review.
  • You use a Mac: BlueStacks or NoxPlayer.
  • You want Google's curated games only: Google Play Games for PC.
  • You build Android apps: Android Studio Emulator.
  • You run Linux: Waydroid.

If you decide MSI's build is right for you after all, the MSI App Player download page has the official installer and setup steps.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best alternative to MSI App Player?

BlueStacks 5 is the closest, because MSI App Player is built with BlueStacks. It adds a Mac version and published requirements and release notes. Google Play Games for PC is the main option from Google.

Is there an MSI App Player for Mac?

MSI lists no Mac version. BlueStacks offers a macOS version for macOS 11 and later, and it runs the same engine family.

Is there an Android emulator that works without virtualization?

Modern Android emulators for games rely on hardware virtualization, and BlueStacks says its 64 bit instances require it. If your PC cannot enable it, cloud play is the realistic option.

Is Google Play Games for PC the same as MSI App Player?

No. Google Play Games runs a curated list of games that Google approves for PC. MSI App Player is a full Android emulator where you can install apps from the Play Store.

Can I use these alternatives on an MSI Claw?

The Claw runs Windows, so Windows emulators can install on it. MSI App Player is the one MSI presents for the Claw and includes Console Mode.
