The closest HWiNFO alternatives are HWMonitor for a simpler sensor view, Libre Hardware Monitor for an open source option, Core Temp for processor temperature alone, CPU-Z for component identification, and AIDA64 Extreme for a paid suite with benchmarks. None of them is listed by its vendor with the same mix of inventory, sensors, alerts, reports and logging that the free HWiNFO edition has.

Why look for an alternative

People search for HWiNFO alternatives for three reasons. The program shows more than they need. The free edition's 12 hour shared memory limit or 5 item on screen display cap gets in the way; both are explained on the free vs Pro page. Or they need the 64 bit build at work and want to compare the cost of a licence with other tools.

The list below sticks to what each vendor states on its official page on 5 November 2026. Where a page gives no price or no version, the entry says so.

Notes on each

HWMonitor and HWMonitor PRO come from CPUID. The standard program is a plain sensor list for Windows x86 and x64. PRO adds remote monitoring of PCs or Android devices over TCP/IP with up to 20 connections and 2 years of free updates. The two are set against HWiNFO in HWiNFO vs HWMonitor.

AIDA64 Extreme is the commercial suite in this list. Its vendor describes detailed hardware and software information, diagnostics and benchmarks, compatibility with all 64 bit Windows editions including Windows 11 and Windows Server 2025, and 97.5 MB of disk space to install. The product page links to a shop and a trial but states no price.

CPU-Z, also from CPUID, is freeware that reports the processor name, codename, process, package and cache levels, the mainboard and chipset, memory type, size, timings and module specifications, and real time clock speeds. It is an identification tool, not a temperature monitor.

Core Temp shows the temperature of each individual core of every processor in the system and supports Intel, AMD and VIA x86 processors on Windows from XP to 11. Libre Hardware Monitor is free software under the MPL 2.0 licence that reads mainboards, Intel and AMD processors, NVIDIA, AMD and Intel graphics cards, drives and network cards.

When to stay with HWiNFO

  • You want inventory and sensors in one program.
  • You need alerts, XML, CSV or HTML reports, or a CSV sensor log without paying.
  • You use Windows on ARM64 or a legacy 32 bit system.
  • You want frequent hardware support updates: HWiNFO had eight stable releases from January to September 2026.

The HWiNFO review weighs these points, and the HWiNFO download page gets you the current version.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best free alternative to HWiNFO?

For a simpler sensor view, HWMonitor from CPUID. For open source, Libre Hardware Monitor under the MPL 2.0 licence. Both read temperatures, voltages, fan speeds and clock speeds according to their own pages.

Is there an open source alternative to HWiNFO?

Yes. Libre Hardware Monitor is free software licensed under MPL 2.0. Its project page says it monitors temperature sensors, fan speeds, voltages, load and clock speeds.

Is AIDA64 better than HWiNFO?

AIDA64 Extreme is a paid suite that includes benchmarks, which HWiNFO's vendor does not advertise as a main feature. HWiNFO is free for non commercial use and publishes its Pro entry prices. No test results stand behind either claim here.

Is CPU-Z the same kind of tool as HWiNFO?

Only partly. CPU-Z identifies the processor, mainboard, chipset and memory and shows clock speeds. It is not presented by its vendor as a temperature or sensor monitor.

Which alternative just shows CPU temperature?

Core Temp. Its vendor describes it as a compact program that shows the temperature of each individual core of every processor in the system.
