In HWiNFO vs HWMonitor, HWiNFO is the broader tool: it adds a full hardware inventory, XML, CSV and HTML reports, alerts, CSV logging and remote monitoring to the sensor view, free for non commercial use. HWMonitor from CPUID is a narrower program that reads the main health sensors: voltages, temperatures, fan speeds and clock speeds. Pick HWMonitor for a quick look and HWiNFO when you need detail, logs or alerts.

The short version

HWiNFO vs HWMonitor is less a contest than a difference in scope. HWMonitor, published by CPUID, describes itself as a hardware monitoring program that reads a PC's main health sensors. HWiNFO describes itself as system information, monitoring and diagnostics, and the sensor view is only half of it.

Both are current. HWMonitor 1.68 is dated 14 September 2026 and HWiNFO 8.54 is dated 30 September 2026. Neither vendor publishes accuracy tests against the other, and none were run for this page.

When HWiNFO is the better pick

  • You want the specifications of every component as well as the sensors.
  • You need a record: the free edition writes sensor logs to CSV and reports to XML, CSV or HTML.
  • You want a warning when a value crosses a threshold.
  • You run Windows on ARM64 or a very old 32 bit system; the portable package covers both.
  • You feed values to gadgets or panels through shared memory, bearing in mind the 12 hour limit of the free edition.

The HWiNFO review goes through these features and the free edition's limits in more detail.

When HWMonitor is the better pick

HWMonitor does one thing: it shows the main health sensors. If all you want is to glance at temperatures, voltages and fan speeds, a program with that scope gives you less to scroll through.

HWMonitor PRO, version 1.59 dated 21 July 2026, adds remote monitoring of other PCs or Android devices over TCP/IP, a graph generator that saves logging graphs as bitmap files, sensors in the system tray and editable sensor labels. Its licence includes unlimited usage duration, up to 20 remote connections and 2 years of free updates. CPUID does not show the price on the product page.

Paid editions compared

HWiNFO publishes its entry prices: from $29 for the first year of a Pro subscription, renewals from $14.50, and a lifetime licence from $199, with refunds accepted within 14 days subject to review. Details are on the HWiNFO Pro price page. For HWMonitor PRO you have to visit CPUID's shop to see a price.

The two paid editions also unlock different things. HWiNFO Pro mainly removes limits and permits commercial use. HWMonitor PRO adds features the free HWMonitor does not have, such as remote monitoring and graphs. Other options are listed under HWiNFO alternatives.

Try HWiNFO first

The free edition already includes logging, alerts and reports. If it turns out to be more than you need, a simpler monitor is one download away.

Frequently asked questions

Is HWiNFO better than HWMonitor?

HWiNFO does more: inventory, reports, alerts, CSV logging and remote monitoring on top of the sensors. HWMonitor is narrower and reads the main health sensors. Which is better depends on whether you want detail or a quick look.

Which is more accurate, HWiNFO or HWMonitor?

Neither vendor publishes accuracy figures and no measurement was made for this page. Both read the sensors that the hardware exposes.

Are both programs free?

HWiNFO64 is free for non commercial use, with paid Pro licences. CPUID offers HWMonitor as a direct download and sells an extended HWMonitor PRO; its product page does not state a price.

Can I run HWiNFO and HWMonitor together?

Neither vendor's product page addresses running both at once. The download page of this guide covers getting HWiNFO; try one monitor at a time if readings look odd.

Does HWMonitor log temperatures?

The standard HWMonitor page does not mention logging. HWMonitor PRO has a graph generator that saves monitoring data and produces logging graphs as bitmap files.
